01

The relativistic two-body problem

How do compact binaries move and radiate?

I study the orbital dynamics and gravitational radiation of compact binaries using analytic approximations. The aim is to connect conservative two-body dynamics with the nonlinear radiative effects that enter gravitational-wave predictions.

The post-Newtonian expansion resolves the slow-motion, weak-field regime. The post-Minkowskian expansion instead organizes interactions in powers of Newton’s constant while retaining relativistic velocity dependence.

02

Post-Newtonian gravity & effective field theory

How can different binary scales be treated consistently?

Nonrelativistic general relativity (NRGR) is an effective field theory that separates the scales of compact objects, orbital motion and radiation. I use this framework to calculate binary interactions and to relate radiation-zone processes to their contributions to the effective action.

My work includes tail contributions for arbitrary electric and magnetic multipoles, and an emission-to-self-energy prescription. Multipolar source actions, dimensional regularization and Ward identities organize and check these calculations.

  • Conservative binary dynamics from gravitational tail emission processes (2023)

03

Gravitational radiation & nonlinear effects

How does emitted radiation affect the waveform and the orbit?

Gravitational waves interact with the background field and with other radiation. I study tails, tails of tails, nonlinear memory and angular-momentum tails, including their contributions to radiative multipoles and binary dynamics.

With collaborators, I calculated multipole renormalization, memory contributions to the waveform and effective action, and radiative contributions to the scattering angle. These calculations connect PN radiation physics to scattering observables in the PN/PM overlap.

04

Post-Minkowskian scattering & multiloop methods

What can a scattering encounter teach us about gravity?

Scattering provides a relativistic setting for the two-body problem. With Yuchen Du, Zhengwen Liu and Hongbin Wang, I calculated the conservative impulse and deflection angle through third post-Minkowskian order in scalar-tensor and Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity.

My contribution included constructing the diagrammatic integrands and evaluating two-loop Feynman integrals. Integration-by-parts (IBP) identities reduce them to master integrals, solved with differential equations and the method of regions. Comparison in the overlapping PN regime checks the result.

05

Gravity beyond general relativity

How would additional gravitational fields change a binary?

Scalar-tensor and Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories make it possible to study departures from general relativity in a controlled setting. I derived the conservative two-body dynamics through 2PN using EFT and, with Shuang-Yong Zhou, studied spinning black holes and spin-dependent binary interactions through 3PN in ESGB gravity.

The program combines black-hole solutions, thermodynamics and worldline EFT. Matching relates the effective point-particle description to the internal properties of compact objects, providing analytic input for gravitational-wave tests of gravity.

Exact solutions, symmetries & integrability

Which geometric structures make Einstein’s equations tractable?

My earlier research at UFPE, with Carlos Batista, investigated exact solutions using commuting Killing vectors and nontrivial Killing tensors. This led to classes of integrable metrics and gauge fields in Einstein–Yang–Mills theory, vacuum solutions with a cosmological constant, and higher-dimensional generalizations.

Differential geometry, hidden symmetries, separability and algebraic classification supplied the foundation for my later work in perturbative gravity.
