<h2 id="gravity">1. Directly relevant to relativistic two-body dynamics and gravitational waves</h2>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>1. Time-domain extreme-mass-ratio scattering waveform through 5PM and 7PN</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Binary black hole scattering in the extreme-mass-ratio limit: time-domain waveform</em><br>
      Andrea Geralico — arXiv:2609.30118 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> Geralico computes the gravitational waveform from nonspinning two-body scattering directly in the time domain, at first order in the mass ratio, through 5PM and 7PN. The calculation complements frequency-domain first-order-self-force treatments and provides extensive symbolic information.</p>
      <p>Important qualification: this is <strong>not</strong> the highest-PM waveform overall. Geralico's earlier frequency-domain work reaches 8PM. The new element here is the high-order <strong>time-domain</strong> waveform and its 7PN depth.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It provides an independent bridge between the PM expansion, self-force theory and directly usable time-domain observables, and gives useful limiting data for future generic-mass-ratio radiative PM calculations.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">Very high relevance</span> Useful as an extreme-mass-ratio benchmark for PM radiation, radiation reaction and B2B-style comparisons.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30118" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.30118</a>
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26614" rel="noopener">Earlier 8PM frequency-domain work</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>2. Soft theorems meet PN tails, memory and RG logarithms</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Classical soft theorems meet the post-Newtonian expansion</em><br>
      Samim Akhtar, Riccardo Sturani — arXiv:2609.29895 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> Akhtar and Sturani analyze emitted gravitational radiation propagating through the static background curvature of a compact binary in the multipolar PN framework. They connect the infrared logarithms associated with memory, tail-of-memory and higher tail insertions to the logarithmic classical soft theorems of Sahoo and Sen.</p>
      <p>They also discuss UV logarithms associated with short-distance sensitivity of the multipole expansion and the corresponding renormalization-group running.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> The paper organizes several hereditary PN structures in a language that may let amplitude/soft information predict logarithmic terms without rederiving every contribution from scratch.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">Very high relevance</span> Particularly close to EFT work on tails, radiation and renormalization.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29895" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.29895</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>3. Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit gains high-PN analytic machinery</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Black Hole Perturbation Toolkit: Low frequency and post-Newtonian expansions</em><br>
      Jakob Neef, Chris Kavanagh, Adrian Ottewill — arXiv:2609.25281 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> New toolkit functionality generates low-frequency/PM expansions of MST coefficients, renormalized angular momentum, radial amplitudes and phase shifts; PN expansions of homogeneous radial functions; and, for circular Kerr orbits, inhomogeneous amplitudes and fluxes at the horizon and infinity.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> Extreme-mass-ratio PN information is increasingly important as boundary data for EOB/PM reconstruction and for checking generic-mass-ratio calculations. Reproducible analytic software lowers the cost of pushing to very high orders.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">High relevance</span> A practical generator of test-mass/high-PN information for PM-derived potentials and gauge-invariant checks.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25281" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.25281</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>4. Quadratic gravitational-wave scattering by Kerr: NR and second-order Teukolsky agree</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Quadratic Gravitational-Wave Scattering by Kerr Black Holes</em><br>
      Lennox S. Keeble, Hengrui Zhu, Lawrence E. Kidder, Harald P. Pfeiffer, Mark A. Scheel — arXiv:2609.25522 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> Nearly monochromatic (2,±2) gravitational waves are scattered off Kerr black holes, and numerical relativity is used to measure the quadratic outgoing (4,4) response at twice the parent frequency. A daughter-mode resonance tracks the fundamental (4,4,0) QNM, and the nonlinear coupling depends on the full scattering state rather than frequency alone.</p>
      <p>A semi-analytic second-order Teukolsky calculation reproduces the NR response.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It advances second-order BH perturbation theory beyond quadratic-QNM studies toward a broader continuum scattering problem.</p>
      <p><span class="label medium">Medium-high relevance</span> More peripheral to PN/PM dynamics, but important for nonlinear GW theory and strong-field response.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25522" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.25522</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>5. A 5PM-informed spinning EOB framework is extended to radiation and evolution</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Evolution dynamics of spinning binaries in effective-one-body theory to fifth Post-Minkowskian order</em><br>
      Jiliang Jing, Sheng Long, Weike Deng, Jieci Wang — arXiv:2609.27251 — published in <em>Science China Physics, Mechanics &amp; Astronomy</em> 69, 110142 (2026)</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> This is best regarded as an extension/application rather than a new independent derivation of the conservative 5PM dynamics. The authors build on their previously constructed effective rotating metric through 5PM, derive the Hamiltonian and stress tensor, formulate a separable sourced Teukolsky-like equation for ψ<sub>4</sub>, and obtain fluxes, radiation reaction and waveforms.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> The methodological interest is in combining high-PM conservative information, perturbations of an effective metric and dissipative EOB dynamics in one framework.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">High relevance</span> Particularly relevant for the question of how PM scattering information should feed EOB waveform models.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27251" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.27251</a>
        <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11433-026-3067-7" rel="noopener">Journal DOI</a>
      </div>

<article class="item">
      <h3>6. Environmental dynamics: EFT renormalization of dynamical friction and ultralight-scalar binaries</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta">
        <em>Renormalized Dynamical Friction in Black-Hole Spacetimes</em> — Jordan Wilson-Gerow — arXiv:2609.23192<br>
        <em>Dynamical Friction in an Ultralight Scalar Medium across Coherent and Stochastic Regimes</em> — Soumodeep Mitra, Jordan Wilson-Gerow — arXiv:2609.28641
      </div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> The first paper formulates environmental/self-force effects through a Schwinger–Keldysh EFT effective action and derives a UV divergence in the environmental-wake contribution together with the required renormalization and matching. The second studies binaries moving in ultralight-scalar media and identifies long-wavelength dissipation, a universal Coulomb logarithm and stochastic fluctuations of the orbit-averaged energy flux.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> Environmental corrections are being recast as a controlled EFT problem involving renormalization, curved-spacetime Green functions and self-force regularization.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">High methodological relevance</span></p>
      <p class="note"><strong>Earlier context.</strong> Wilson-Gerow’s first paper was submitted on 19 September, just before this issue’s reading window; it is included as context for the related work discussed here.</p>
      <div class="links">

<article class="item">
      <h3>7. A compact analytic method for periastron advance generates new finite-size terms</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Periastron advance from the perturbed Binet equation</em><br>
      Quentin Henry — arXiv:2609.29907 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> Poincaré–Lindstedt perturbation theory is applied directly to the Binet equation, with secular terms determining the radial-frequency shift and hence periastron advance. The approach handles polynomial, logarithmic and inverse-power perturbations and multiple simultaneous expansion parameters.</p>
      <p>The paper reproduces known compact-binary results and derives new terms from arbitrary mass/current tidal multipoles, spin-induced multipoles, NNLO current-quadrupole tides and several electromagnetic corrections.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It offers a compact route from conservative dynamics to a gauge-invariant observable.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">High relevance</span> Useful as an independent check on Hamiltonians, potentials and EOB information.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29907" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.29907</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<h2 id="qft">2. EFT, amplitudes, QFT methods, and Feynman integrals</h2>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>8. Hidden Region Finder addresses missed regions in Minkowskian Feynman-integral asymptotics</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Hidden Region Finder: asymptotic expansions of Feynman integrals in Minkowski space</em><br>
      Einan Gardi — arXiv:2609.29442 — preprint, CERN-TH-2026-233</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> Standard geometric method-of-regions implementations work cleanly in Euclidean limits, where regions map to facets of a Newton polytope. Minkowskian limits can contain additional hidden regions. Gardi relates these systematically to pinch singularities and Landau conditions and proposes an algorithm to identify them.</p>
      <p>The work tests this structure on massless four-, five- and six-point integrals in wide-angle on-shell, planar, collinear, double-collinear and Regge limits.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> Missing an asymptotic region is more dangerous than merely having a difficult integral. A robust criterion for hidden contributions could substantially improve automated multiloop expansions.</p>
      <p><span class="label high">Very high relevance</span> Directly relevant to PM/classical-gravity integrals in Minkowskian and threshold/Regge-like kinematics.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29442" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.29442</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>9. Six-point consistency strongly constrains the Veneziano amplitude</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Six-point consistency and uniqueness of the Veneziano amplitude</em><br>
      Ilmo Sung — arXiv:2609.29919 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> In a specified four-dimensional maximally supersymmetric planar setting, six-point consistency, SUSY Ward identities, meromorphicity, positivity and an unsubtracted forward dispersion relation constrain the four-point amplitude very strongly, forcing an infinite equally spaced tower of mass-squared poles under the assumptions used.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> This is an amplitude-bootstrap result in which higher-point consistency is used to reconstruct highly rigid lower-point analytic structure.</p>
      <p><span class="label medium">Moderate relevance</span> Structurally interesting for modern amplitude methods, though not directly a classical-gravity calculation.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29919" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.29919</a>
      </div>
    </article>

    
<article class="item">
      <h3>10. Light-ring ladder symmetry organizes QNMs, Regge poles and greybody factors</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Accurate Black Hole Quasinormal Modes, Regge Poles, and Greybody Factors from Light Ring Ladder Symmetry</em><br>
      Rajes Ghosh, David Pereñiguez, Jaime Redondo-Yuste, Emanuele Berti — arXiv:2609.25228 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> The authors identify a ladder symmetry in the high-frequency/light-ring expansion that maps the BH perturbation problem to an anharmonic-oscillator calculation. They obtain Schwarzschild QNMs, Regge poles and greybody factors analytically through 18th order in inverse angular momentum, with resummation.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It unifies several manifestations of black-hole response within one calculational framework and may generalize to rotation and modified gravity.</p>
      <p><span class="label medium">Moderate-high relevance</span> Particularly interesting from the scattering/black-hole-response perspective.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25228" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.25228</a>

      <h3>11. Can asymptotic safety generate a Horndeski sector?</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Emergence of Horndeski gravity from asymptotic safety?</em><br>
      Astrid Eichhorn, Pedro G. S. Fernandes, Fabian Willaschek — arXiv:2609.29369 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> The authors study two nonminimal scalar-gravity interactions generated in an asymptotic-safety setting. They lie in the Horndeski combination only for a particular coupling ratio, which their analysis can realize for a special cosmological-constant value. They also find indications of approximate radiative stability.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It attempts to connect a UV quantum-gravity scenario to a restricted infrared scalar-tensor operator structure.</p>
      <p><strong>Qualification.</strong> The result is a hint within the chosen truncation, not a proof that asymptotic safety uniquely generates Horndeski gravity.</p>
      <p><span class="label medium">Moderate relevance</span> Unusually close to scalar-tensor gravity among quantum-gravity papers.</p>
      <div class="links">

<article class="item">
      <h3>12. Quantum gravity obstructs an exact extremal endpoint in a black-hole collapse construction</h3>
      <div class="paper-meta"><em>Collapse to extremality: The third law of black hole thermodynamics in quantum gravity</em><br>
      Gustavo J. Turiaci, Chih-Hung Wu — arXiv:2609.26875 — preprint</div>
      <p><strong>What happened.</strong> The paper constructs a classical process apparently driving a near-extremal charged black hole to extremality in finite time, then analyzes the same setup quantum mechanically using the near-horizon AdS<sub>2</sub> description. The sharp extremal endpoint is replaced by an energy distribution whose probability vanishes at exact extremality and peaks at positive excitation energy.</p>
      <p><strong>Why it matters.</strong> It gives a concrete dynamical example in which the classical and quantum versions of the third law behave differently.</p>
      <p><span class="label medium">Indirect relevance</span> Conceptually useful for black-hole thermodynamics and quantum gravity.</p>
      <div class="links">
        <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26875" rel="noopener">arXiv:2609.26875</a>
      </div>
    </article>
